About the journal

The desk behind the road maps

By Editorial RoadTripRoutes · Last reviewed September 18, 2026

TL;DR

RoadTripRoutes is an independent, informational guide to road trips and weekend getaways across the United States. We publish real routes with leg-by-leg budgets, numbered stop maps and honest caveats — every figure dated and linked to an official source.

What this site is

A road journal, not a booking engine and not a listicle machine. We start with the roads Americans actually take — the leg you’ll drive, the stop you’ll make, the ticket you’ll pay — and we build each guide the way a good trip is planned: in order, with numbers, and with the thing nobody mentioned before you left.

What this site is not

  • Not a booking or affiliate storefront — there is nothing to “buy now” on RoadTripRoutes.
  • Not a paraphraser of other travel sites — every route gets its own angle and its own “what nobody tells you” note.
  • Not a promise-maker — travel prices change, and we say so, in every guide and on every sourced figure.

How we work

RoadTripRoutes is written by a small editorial desk led by a lifelong American road traveler. We read the official sources first — NPS fee pages, AAA fuel data, state DOT and toll-authority pages — and we publish what we can stand behind:

  • Everything is dated. Each figure carries a “as of” date and a link to its source.
  • Estimates are labeled. When we estimate lodging bands or seasonal averages, we say it’s an estimate — never a fake precision.
  • We verify on the ground where we can. The desk has driven many of the miles we write about. Where a route depends on public data we haven’t personally covered, we say that plainly too.
  • We update the numbers. Guides are reviewed at least twice a year; corrections and fee changes are noted with a new “last reviewed” date.

Who signs the articles

All guides are signed Editorial RoadTripRoutes — a stable author of record for the whole desk, the way a newspaper column carries the masthead. That one signature keeps accountability where it belongs: articles aren’t orphaned content floating under fake “staff writer” names you can’t pin anyone to. The person behind the signature is a traveler who has planned road trips in the Southwest, the Pacific coast, the Blue Ridge and beyond — and who got tired of guides that tell you where to sleep and never what the drive costs.

Plan ahead

We also know what we don’t know. If a specific road segment, bridge or park facility matters to your trip, confirm it on the official pages — closures, fire seasons and toll changes happen faster than any editorial calendar.

When we get something wrong

We fix it, quickly and visibly. Send the correction to hello@roadtriproutes.com with the page and the figure, and we’ll verify against the official source and update with a dated note. The editorial guidelines spell out the corrections policy; the process for how budget figures are calculated is public too.

A note on independence

RoadTripRoutes is not affiliated with NPS, AAA, DOT, or any state or federal agency, and we accept no payment for coverage. The site is ad-supported information: a commercial interest in keeping the lights on, never a reason to inflate a number or bury a caveat.

— Signed, Editorial RoadTripRoutes
