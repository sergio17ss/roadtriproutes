Editorial guidelines

The standards we hold ourselves to

By Editorial RoadTripRoutes · Last reviewed September 18, 2026

TL;DR

Every guide is original, sourced and dated. We never pay for placement, accept nothing that changes a number, and route every correction through the public process below. If a brand doesn’t survive our standards, the brand goes.

Mission

RoadTripRoutes exists to help people plan the drive, not just the destination. That means real routes with leg-by-leg budgets, numbered stop maps, and the overlooked details that make a trip work or quietly ruin it. Success is a reader who leaves with a trip they can count, not a tab-full of tabs.

Accuracy & sourcing

  1. Official or primary sources first: AAA, NPS, state DOTs, toll authorities, USDOT/FHWA. If it’s a number, it gets a source and a date.
  2. Everything is dated. A figure without an “as of” date is an estimate and is labeled as one.
  3. Ranges, not false precision. We say $140–$240, not $197.33.
  4. When we disagree with an official figure, we say why — but we check the official figure first, always.
  5. The full math is public in the methodology.

Originality & voice

  1. No paraphrased SERP. Every route develops its own angle, its own leg structure and its own “what nobody tells you” note.
  2. No AI listicle filler, no “7 hidden gems you’ll never find (read to believe)”. If we can’t verify it, we don’t publish it.
  3. The voice is a practical traveler: experienced, warm, direct. We say “skip the exit” plainly, and we say “we don’t know yet” equally plainly.
  4. We never promise an outcome — not a price, not a crowd-free viewpoint, not a guarantee of anything on a road.

Advertising & independence

  • The site is ad-supported through a contextual network. Advertising is programmatic and never placed for a fee by a subject of an article.
  • No pay-for-placement, ever. A hotel, park, brand or DMO cannot buy a mention, a ranking or a favorable reading of a number.
  • If a guide eventually links partners, every link will carry a visible affiliate disclosure — and it will never sit inside a recommendation we wouldn’t have made anyway.
  • Sponsored content would be clearly labeled “Sponsored” and excluded from routes and budgets. We publish none today.

Corrections policy

  1. Report: email hello@roadtriproutes.com with the page URL and the figure you believe is wrong, ideally with the official source you checked.
  2. Verify: we check against the official source within 5 working days; if the figure is wrong, we fix the page and the source list.
  3. Record: a dated correction note is appended to the bottom of the page, and the “last reviewed” date advances. Mistakes are fixed visibly, not silently.
  4. Escalation: for unverified or disputed claims we publish our source and date so the reader can judge with us.

Images

  • Photos are licensed (Unsplash etc.) and credited to their source page on every use, with descriptive alt text.
  • We never strip a credit, crop a license away, or pass a stock photo off as our own fieldwork.
  • Where a photo is ours, it is captioned as ours.

Review & currency

Guides are re-checked at least twice a year; fees, tolls and closures are the usual suspects. Each guide shows its “last reviewed” date, and when something big changes — a park-fee increase, a bridge toll, a permanent closure — we re-check on the day we hear about it.

Your role in this

The standards above are only as strong as the watch. Use the website, question the numbers, send the correction. That’s how a road journal stays honest — talk to us.

— Editorial RoadTripRoutes
